##### Abstract

The present study aims to further our understanding of Large Language Models’ (LLMs) capabilities
of simulating human participants as subjects of social science research. Building on previous work,
this paper introduces a customized framework for the simulation of threads – topic discussions – on
the social media platform Reddit, using LLMs. The simulation framework unifies statistical models
of Reddit user behavior and the generative capabilities of LLMs to govern the decision-making and
behavior of simulated agents.
Using demographics and survey data, ranging from self-reported online behavior to knowledge of
and interest in diverse political topics, this study created a sample of 180 unique simulation agents.
These agents were then divided into two subgroups and set to discuss 20 different political topics. In
order to judge the framework’s ability to faithfully reproduce human behavior, given the context, its
performance on three key aspects was analyzed: the ability to simulate different individual users, the
simulated patterns of interaction, and the recreation of Reddit-specific discussion network structures.
According to these three aspects, the simulated discussions were analyzed and compared to empirical
data generated by a recent experiment on Reddit. The simulated agents were shown to over-portray
human activity with regard to commenting behavior and to struggle with accurately memorizing
the discussion history. At the same time, the simulation was able to reproduce temporal patterns of
human social media interaction. With the present work, I explore the current possibilities of using
LLMs as subjects in the simulation of human online discourse.
